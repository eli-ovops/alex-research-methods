Israelov and Nielsen’s 2015 FAJ article decomposes covered calls into passive equity, short volatility and dynamic equity exposure. Its main tables span 25 March 1996–31 December 2014. Table 1 reports 5.9% simple annualized excess return for a simulated ATM overwrite; Table 6 reports 4.9% for the BXM index, both above three-month US LIBOR. Neither is a verified personal all-cost cash ledger. Note 9 attributes most of their difference to expiration-day methodology and unavailable historical intraday option prices, not simply commissions. The paper warns that dynamic hedging increases trading costs. OptionMetrics/Black–Scholes deltas and full-sample average exposure support the authors’ attribution, not an independently replicated tradable forecasting advantage. The authors disclose commercial interests. This review obtained no raw dataset, code package, complete trade/fee ledger or post-publication out-of-sample evidence; it does not establish their global absence. Existing historical 20ETH cash evidence and current personal-access unknowns remain unchanged.
