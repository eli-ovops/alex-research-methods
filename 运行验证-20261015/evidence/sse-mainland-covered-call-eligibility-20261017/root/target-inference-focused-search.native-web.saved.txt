### Demystifying the dataset: A Guide to Understanding Optiver - Trading at the Close

Here we are, presented with another chance to delve into machine learning. This competition is titled "Trading at the Close," and it's hosted by Optiver, a market maker. The objective of this competition is to predict the future price movement of stocks in comparison to a synthetic Nasdaq stock index's future price movement. To accomplish this, we'll utilize historical data from the daily ten-minute closing auctions.

Let’s discuss my initial challenges. Understanding the features wasn’t a walk in the park for me, even with Optiver’s fantastic tutorial kernel and resources. Therefore, I’m here to provide an additional explanation that I hope will assist others.

Optiver gives us access to a training dataset containing 17 columns and more than 5.2 million rows. This data represents a fusion of the order book from continuous trading and the auction book. The opening and closing prices are determined through an auction system, just like in nearly all organized markets

Here is the procedure:

> At 4 pm Nasdaq calculates the price that will maximize the number of shares matches based on closed orders (MOC, LOC, IO) and continuous market orders and execute the cross at a single price called the Nasdaq Official Close Price (NOCP).

Ok at this point of the movie I was totally lost, but bear with me. Keep these three point in mind:

  1. The order book and the auction book are not the same.
  2. The closing price comes from the merging of both books.
  3. The price will maximize the number of shares matches from both books. This is important.

Now, let's get into some of the features in the training set using an example from the order and auction book.

If you are new to this, the order book is essentially a list of buying and selling orders for stocks at different price levels.
In this case, the imbalance would be 1 lot, in the sell direction.

Going back to our training set, you can find:

  * Imbalance Size ('imbalance_size') and the Imbalance Buy-Sell Flag: This flag indicates the direction of the imbalance, with 'buy' represented as 1, 'sell' as -1, and 'no imbalance' as 0.
  * Near Price: This is the near indicative clearing price, calculated to maximize the number of shares matched based on close orders (auction book) and continuous market orders, just 5 minutes before the closing cross.
  * Far Price: The far price information pertains to the crossing price that maximizes the number of shares matched based on closing interest only, excluding continuous market orders
  * Reference Price: The reference price is the price within the Nasdaq Best Bid and Offer (BBO) where paired shares are maximized, the imbalance is minimized, and the distance from the midpoint is minimized, in that order.
  * Weighted Average Price (WAP): This WAP considers both the bid and ask prices, weighted by their respective sizes. This weighted average reflects the average price at which trades are likely to occur based on the available buy and sell orders. Image

The last thing left for us to understand is our target variable, the one we aim to predict, is the change in the weighted average price (WAP) of the stock over the next 60 seconds, minus the change in the weighted average price of the synthetic index over the same 60 seconds. The unit of measurement for this target variable is basis points (bps). To clarify, 1% corresponds to 100 bps, and 1 bps is equivalent to a 0.01% price move. Image

I hope this post can assist you in gaining a better understanding of this competition. There are more posts on the forum covering similar topics, and honestly, the kernel provided by Optiver was quite helpful. So, this post might not introduce anything new. However, it was valuable for me to write it as it helped clarify and comprehend certain aspects of the data. Whether it's redundant or not, I don't mind. If you find yourself in a similar situation, I encourage you to do the same. It does take time and effort, but it pays off.

(Btw this fix improved CV but worsened public LB score. Idk why LOL)

Also, we wrote our own functions for RSI, MACD and bollinger bands indicators as the public one gave inaccurate results in inference

What worked (impact on CV)

  * Features based on revealed targets. We used lags 1,2,3 for target grouped by stock_id and seconds_in_bucket as features into our models (-0.005)
  * Signed representation of imbalance_size (-0.003)
  * Continuous model training with revealed targets. We refitted our LGB and CatBoost model at fixed intervals (elaborated later) (-0.006)
  * Performing CV in streaming fashion. To do this, we saved the data each time period in _.csv and delivered them one by one in chronological order when calculating CV. This took much longer but correlated better with the public LB
  * Zero sum post processing (-0.005) (but we are not sure in private LB so we only chose 1 submission with this)
  * Global Features (-0.004). We have to re-initialize these values this whenever we want to retrain the model to keep it up to date
  * Technical indicators RSI, MACD and bollinger bands. We had to rewrite them for good results (-0.002)

What did not work (impact on CV)

  * Group by date_id and stock_id for rolling features instead of stock_id (+0.003). We didn’t do this in the end
  * Lagged features shift(x) where x is large. Made CV better but worsened LB
  * Rolling features over a window x where x is large. Made CV better but worsened LB
  * Sector features (+0.002)
  * Neural Networks
  * Triplet Imbalances (+0.001) realised this feature gave very unstable values because of precision issues so we decided to discard this even though they improved the public LB score (worsened from 5.3315 to 5.3327)
  * Dropping features based on feature importances
  * Zero mean postprocessing. It makes our ensembles and LB/CV correlation worse for some reason so we didn’t choose this. In fact the ensemble does worse with zero mean (5.333) compared to zero sum (5.3327)

First of all, I would like to extend my gratitude to Optiver and Kaggle for organizing this competition. I also appreciate the great notebooks and discussions which I learned a lot from.
I would like to congratulate all the winners.
I will share my solution hoping it can be helpful for someone. Since this is first time post, please forgive me if it is difficult to read or understand.

## Summary

I used 3 different feature sets. Each set has common basic features such as and I trained both LightGBM and CatBoost on each feature set and ensembled these models. For each feature set and each method (LightGBM or CatBoost), “overall” model and “every 10 seconds” model were built and selected the better one for each 10-seconds i.e., each seconds_in_bucket value.

## Pre-processing and Preparation

I divided stock_id into 7 groups by using KMeans method. The total cluster number 7 was determined by elbow method.

## Feature Engineering

Main features I created are below:

  * Common features: raw features, doublet imbalance features, triplet imbalance features
  * Set 1: imbalance momentum, price pressure, depth pressure, some statistics of each price features and size features, z-score in the same knn group, shifted value for z-score and size features, differences for price features and revealed target, day of week, seconds, minute
  * Set2: z-score and difference with median in the same seconds_in_bucket value for [bid_size, ask_size, ask_price, bid_price, wap, revealed target], differences and fractional change for price features and size features
  * Set 3: moving average for price features and size features and revealed target

Please see appendix at the end of this text for details.

## Validation Strategy

Simple time-based split was used for validation. The first 435 days were used for training and the last 45 days were used for validation. All models were re-trained on all data with the iteration number determined by validation.

  * `[bid/ask]_price` - Price of the most competitive buy/sell level in the non-auction book.
  * `[bid/ask]_size` - The dollar notional amount on the most competitive buy/sell level in the non-auction book.
  * `wap` - The weighted average price in the non-auction book.

\frac{BidPrice\ast AskSize+AskPrice\ast BidSize}{BidSize+AskSize}

$$\frac{ {BidPrice * AskSize + AskPrice * BidSize}}{BidSize + AskSize} $$
  * `seconds_in_bucket` - The number of seconds elapsed since the beginning of the day's closing auction, always starting from 0.
  * `target` - The 60 second future move in the wap of the stock, less the 60 second future move of the synthetic index. Only provided for the train set.
    * The synthetic index is a custom weighted index of Nasdaq-listed stocks constructed by Optiver for this competition.
    * The unit of the target is basis points, which is a common unit of measurement in financial markets. A 1 basis point price move is equivalent to a 0.01% price move.
    * Where t is the time at the current observation, we can define the target:

$$ Target = (\frac{StockWAP_{t+60}}{StockWAP_{t}} - \frac{IndexWAP_{t+60}}{IndexWAP_{t}}) * 10000 $$

All size related columns are in USD terms.

All price related columns are converted to a price move relative to the stock wap (weighted average price) at the beginning of the auction period.

One final submission was a stack where LGBM was trained on top of the predictions of CAT and XGB and the other final submission was an ensemble with equal weights. Both submissions were post processed by taking all predictions from a single client (credit card) and replacing them with that client's average prediction. This PP increased LB by 0.001.

### Details of the submission

#### Time is not most important

After Microsoft Malware, I was reluctant to enter another time series competition. To my relief, this competition actually isn't about time. The reason adversarial validation has AUC=1 is not because the nature of fraud changes radically over time but rather because the clients in the dataset change radically over time.

What was special about the submission

An approach that was somehow creative, impactful, or otherwise worth noting. For example, things that were non-obvious, or that very few thought to do.

Once you realize this, you will know that the challenge in this competition is building a model that can predict unseen clients (not unseen time).
